# How accurate is the matching?

Measured against **3438 labeled pairs** of a device and a real FDA recall (589 built from 48 real recalls, plus 2858 unrelated pairs between them), with the right answer decided before the matcher is asked. Generated by `npm run eval`; `npm test` fails when this page is out of date.

**What this is, and is not.** The recalls are real openFDA records (frozen in `eval/recalls.json`). The devices were made from what each recall says (its maker and model, a serial or lot it lists, its UDI-DI, a range it states) and from near misses (another model, another maker, another UDI-DI, a lot code that looks like a range), so the right answer follows from the recall text: it is written down in `scripts/make-eval-set.mjs`. The labels were written by the developer, not by a clinical engineer, and the devices are not a real hospital's. So this is a regression set and a sanity check of how the rules read real recall wording, **not** a measure of how often the app is right in a hospital.

## Recalls with several products, recalls that leave units out, and makers with two names

- **Several products in one recall** (6 real recalls whose code text names the model, UDI-DI and lots of every product: `Z-3140-2026`, `Z-2987-2026`, `Z-0158-2027`, `Z-3015-2026`, `Z-2900-2026`, `Z-0420-2024`): 120 pairs, 116 right (96.7%). In 27 of them the model or UDI-DI is right but the lot or serial is one the recall lists for a *different* product of the same recall; the matcher never called those *exact* (27 of 27).
- **Makers that go by two names** (9 real recalls where the hospital might write the maker differently from the firm the FDA filed the recall under: "Hill-Rom" for Baxter, "BD" for CareFusion, "Hospira" for ICU Medical, "GE HealthCare" for Datex-Ohmeda): 31 pairs, 31 right (100.0%). The short list of company families is in `src/makerAliases.js`; without it these pairs were *possible* or not found.
- **Recalls that leave units out** (3 real recalls: `Z-0420-2024`, `Z-1505-2024`, `Z-1884-2024`): 44 pairs. Of the 7 units in them that the text really covers, 6 came out *likely* and 1 *exact* (a unit listed in an entry that has no exclusion of its own): where an entry excludes units the matcher does not read its lists, ranges or "all units" (it would be guessing which units are left out), so a person decides, and a *likely* match still gets a task. Of the 7 units the text rules out, 0 came out *exact*.

## Known limits (what these numbers do not cover)

- **Very short serials without a UDI-DI.** A serial or lot of fewer than four characters ("209") is not trusted on its own, because such a number turns up in recall texts by chance (`MIN_LOT_LENGTH` in `src/matching.js`). A pair like that is *likely*, not *exact*, even when the recall lists the unit. With the UDI-DI recorded the same unit is *exact* (the `udi-listed-unit` cases).
- **Units a recall rules out.** When a recall states a serial range and the unit is outside it, or the text names the unit as excluded ("Excluding 90425438"), the matcher still says *likely* for the product: it does not rule units out, because a wrongly read range would hide a real recall. A person decides, and the reason names what it found.
- **Recalls that leave units out are never read as exact.** Where the text says "except", "excluding" and the like, the matcher switches its unit rules off for that product (a list, a range or "all units" cannot be trusted when the text also names units that are left out). So an affected unit in such a recall is *likely*, not *exact*, and an open-ended bound ("Serial Number before K17160001") is not read at all. These pairs are the under-claims listed above; none is missed, because *likely* also gets a task.
- **Several products: entries are found by their labels.** One product's entry is cut at a label with its number ("Model No", "Part No.", "REF", "Product Code") or at a list number ("2.", "3)"). A recall that starts each entry with a bare number and a name and no label ("5855300 Slim-Cath ... Lot Number: REKY1364") cannot be cut, and there a lot of one product can still be read as another's. No case in this set has that form, so the number above says nothing about it.
- **This measures the matcher, not the search.** Every pair is a device and ONE recall that is already in hand. Whether the right recalls are fetched from openFDA in the first place (by manufacturer, by model and by UDI-DI, in a three-year window, up to 100 per search) is not measured here; a search that was capped is flagged on the dashboard.
- **Makers are compared by their words and by a short list of company families.** "BD", "CareFusion", "Bard" and "Becton Dickinson" are one family; so are GE, Datex-Ohmeda and GE HealthCare, Baxter, Hill-Rom and Welch Allyn, ICU Medical and Hospira, and about fifteen others (`src/makerAliases.js`). A company that is not on the list is compared by its words, as before, so a hospital that writes a maker's old or brand name still needs its UDI-DI or its model number in the recall text to be found. The list is mine, from public acquisition history, not a registry: a missing company costs a match, a wrong one is still checked against the model, the serial and the kind of device.
- **Wording nobody wrote a rule for.** The set holds the 48 recalls above and the ways they write serials, lots and UDI-DIs. A recall that lists its units in a picture, a link or a PDF cannot be read at all, and is *likely* at best.
- **Not a field measurement.** Labels come from the recall text, written by the developer. A hospital's own device list (typos, old model names, missing serials) will do worse than this set; the number to trust is the direction, not the last decimal.

## Re-running it

```bash
node scripts/make-eval-set.mjs --pull   # fetch the recalls added to the list in that script (one request each; the others stay frozen)
node scripts/make-eval-set.mjs          # rebuild the cases from them
npm run eval                            # measure and rewrite this page
```
